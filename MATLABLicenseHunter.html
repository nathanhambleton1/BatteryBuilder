LIVE STATS

  

  







  
HOW IT WORKS

  

    

      
WHAT THIS IS

      
We share a small number of MATLAB seats. When they are all taken, MATLAB just shows a licensing error and closes, and you are left trying again and again by hand.

      
The License Hunter does the retrying for you. It keeps launching MATLAB every few seconds until it gets a seat. As soon as MATLAB opens, MATLAB automatically runs License Acquisition, a small window that grabs the toolboxes you need (Simulink, Simscape, …) and waits for any that are busy.

      
Every attempt, by everyone, is written to the shared LicenseData folder. That is where all the numbers on this page come from, so over time the team can see when during the day MATLAB is easiest to get.

      
Stats only count the work day, 6 AM to 6 PM, Monday to Friday for the week view.

    

    

      
HOW THE % IS COUNTED

      
Counting every launch would make MATLAB look much harder to get than it is. Say you get MATLAB first try at 8:00, then later spend 30 minutes retrying every minute while it's busy: that's 1 success out of 34 launches, but MATLAB was free for part of that time that nobody checked.

      
So the % is counted in 5-minute slots instead. Each slot that anyone launched in counts once, and it counts as free if any launch in it got MATLAB. In the example that's 1 free slot out of 7, not 1 of 34.

      
The day is shown in 30-minute blocks, and each block pools the slots from every day. So a day counts in proportion to how long someone actually tried: one lucky launch is 1 slot, a full half hour of trying is 6. A lucky 100% day plus a 40% full-half-hour day gives about 49%, not the 70% you'd get by averaging the two days.

      
Blocks with less than 15 minutes of data in total are drawn faded, and best/worst ignore them, so one lucky launch can't show up as the best time of day.

      
First try is the other fair number: of all the hunts, how many got MATLAB on their very first launch, i.e. your chance when you sit down and need it.

      
Nobody checks while they're using MATLAB, so some time is never sampled. Survey mode fills those gaps.

      
Every number on the stats page is broken down in The math behind every number.

    

    

      
THE MATH BEHIND EVERY NUMBER

      
Example: the 8:00–8:30 AM block

      
Mon: someone launches once at 8:05 and gets MATLAB. Tue: someone spends the whole half hour trying and gets it in 2 of the 6 slots.

      

      
      

      
Ten denied launches inside the same 5 minutes still count as one busy slot. One launch that gets in makes the whole slot free. Everything below only counts 6 AM–6 PM.

      
Every number, and how it's worked out

      

      

        
NumberCalculation

        
Free %free slots ÷ slots tried, over all time. A slot is 5 minutes of one day. It's tried if anyone launched MATLAB in it (got / denied / full) or the license server was checked in it, and free if any of those launches got MATLAB or any server check showed a seat open (license server).

        
Availability by half hourSame formula, but only the slots in that 30-minute block, pooled across every day (see the example above). The small number under each bar is how many slots were tried. Faded bars have under 3 slots (15 minutes) of data.

        
Week by half hourSame as the bar chart, split by weekday: only slots on that weekday in that block. Faded = under 15 minutes of data.

        
Last 14 work daysSame formula, only that day's slots. Bar length = slots tried that day, so a long bar means lots of data.

        
Best / worst half hourThe block with the highest / lowest bar, only counting blocks with at least 3 tried slots (until no block has that many yet). On a tie, the block with more data wins.

        
First tryhunts whose 1st launch got MATLAB ÷ hunts whose 1st launch got MATLAB or was denied. A hunt starts when you press H. Each survey probe counts as a one-launch hunt. Timeouts and errors are left out.

        
LaunchesCount of every time the hunter started MATLAB: got it + denied + timeout + error.

        
Got MATLABCount of launches that got a seat.

        
Avg hunt waitFor each hunt that got in: time from pressing H to the launch that got MATLAB. Then the average. Survey probes aren't hunts, so they're left out. Median / longest use the same list.

        
Avg sessionFor each MATLAB session the hunter watched end: time from getting MATLAB to MATLAB closing. Then the average.

        
UsersNumber of different usernames with at least one logged attempt.

        
Launches todayLaunches since midnight today, with how many of them got MATLAB. In the PowerShell window, You today counts only your own.

        
Per userThe same numbers worked out from one person's rows only. MATLAB time is the total of their session lengths.

        
Toolbox: requestsOne request = one toolbox asked for in one MATLAB session, started 6 AM–6 PM.

        
Toolbox: got %requests that got the toolbox ÷ requests.

        
Toolbox: free right awayrequests that got it within 5 s ÷ requests, i.e. a seat was open when asked. The by-hour grid is the same, split by the hour the request started.

        
Toolbox: avg / longest waitSeconds from asking to getting the toolbox, only counting requests that got it.

HOW TO USE

  

    

      
START THE HUNTER

      
The normal way

      
    
        
  1. Open the shared folder that holds this page.
    2. 
        
  2. Right-click GrabMatlabLicense.ps1 → Run with PowerShell (Windows 11: click Show more options first).
    3. 
        
  3. The dashboard opens and starts hunting. Leave the window open; it beeps when you get MATLAB.
    4. 
      

      
If your PC blocks scripts

      
If the window flashes and closes, or you see a red message about scripts being disabled:

      
    
        
  1. Open PasteIntoPowerShell.txt and press Ctrl+A then Ctrl+C.
    2. 
        
  2. Open PowerShell (Start menu → type PowerShell → Enter).
    3. 
        
  3. Right-click inside the PowerShell window to paste, then press Enter.
    4. 
      

      
After the first time

      
Either way puts a MATLAB License Hunter shortcut on your desktop. Just double-click that from now on.

    

    

      
THE LICENSE ACQUISITION WINDOW

      
Once MATLAB opens, a License Acquisition window appears inside MATLAB on its own.

      
    
        
  • Tick the toolboxes you need. Simscape Battery is unchecked by default; tick it only if you need it, so it stays free for others.
    • 
        
  • Start grabs the ticked toolboxes. If one is busy it keeps waiting and grabs it as soon as a seat frees up. Stop stops waiting.
    • 
        
  • Release All & Exit closes MATLAB and gives everything back (it asks first; save your work).
    • 
        
  • Toolbox Stats opens team-wide stats for each toolbox, the same numbers as the Toolboxes panel on this page.
    • 
      

    

    

      
GIVING LICENSES BACK

      
MATLAB cannot hand back a single toolbox. Once you have Simulink, you keep it until MATLAB closes. Everything is returned when MATLAB exits, so when you're done for a while, please close MATLAB so someone else can use the seat. Any of these work:

      
    
        
  • Press R in the hunter window and confirm the popup.
    • 
        
  • Click Release All & Exit in the License Acquisition window.
    • 
        
  • Just close MATLAB normally.
    • 
      

      
Save your work first. K (force-kill) does not ask MATLAB nicely, and anything unsaved is lost.

    

    

      
STARTING UP AND SMART HUNTING

      
When the hunter opens it asks what you want to do: H hunt, S survey, or Esc to just watch the stats. (If MATLAB is already open on your PC it skips the question and watches that MATLAB.)

      
With the license server set up, hunting is smart. Instead of opening and killing MATLAB over and over, it waits until the server (corrected, see When the server is wrong) shows a free seat, then launches. Because the server can still be wrong:

      
    
        
  • The first launch of a hunt always happens right away.
    • 
        
  • While the server says it's full, it still tries one test launch every 3 minutes, in case the server is wrong.
    • 
        
  • After a denied launch it waits for the used count to drop (someone leaving) before trying again.
    • 
        
  • If the server hasn't been read in the last 2 minutes, it goes back to plain retrying every few seconds.
    • 
      

      
The status line says what it's waiting for, for example full (34 used, ~34 usable), test launch in 2m 10s.

    

    

      
SURVEY MODE

      
Survey mode is only for collecting data. You never get to keep MATLAB. Press S in the hunter window (or start it with -Survey) and it repeats this every minute:

      
    
        
  1. Launch MATLAB and log whether it got a seat.
    2. 
        
  2. If it did, License Acquisition checks every toolbox once (Battery too) and logs which were free.
    3. 
        
  3. Kill MATLAB straight away so the seats go back, then wait for the next probe.
    4. 
      

      
Leave it running on a spare PC to fill in the hours nobody else checks. Press S again to stop. The gap between probes is set with -SurveyIntervalSeconds (default 60).

    

    

      
LICENSE SERVER SETUP

      
The hunter can read the real seat counts straight from the MATLAB license server, the same numbers the license-watcher website (phpLicenseWatcher) shows. Reading them needs no special access. Once it's set up:

      
    
        
  • The License server panel on the Live page and the Server line in the hunter show how many MATLAB seats are in use right now, and who has them.
    • 
        
  • Whichever hunter is open checks the server about once a minute and shares the result with everyone (LicenseData\server\), so the server isn't asked by every PC.
    • 
        
  • Every check also counts toward the % charts: a 5-minute slot counts as free if a seat was open in it. That fills in the times nobody was trying, so the charts get far more data.
    • 
      

      
When the server is wrong

      
The server's own numbers are often off: it may say 34 of 49 seats are used while nobody can get in, because some seats are reserved for other groups or the counts are stale. So the hunter checks the server against what really happens:

      
    
        
  • Every launch and toolbox request made while the server was being read is a test. Server said 34 used and we were denied means the real limit is at most 34. It said 30 used and we got in means it's at least 31.
    • 
        
  • It picks the limit that explains the most tests from the last 30 days, and shows it as real limit ~33 (hunter) or a crossed-out total with ~33 (this page). Hover a row here to see the likely range and how many tests it's based on.
    • 
        
  • Until something has been denied at least once, the server's total is trusted as is. The more people hunt, the better the correction gets.
    • 
        
  • The % charts use the corrected numbers too.
    • 
      

      
Way 1: the license-watcher website (easiest)

      
    
        
  1. Open the license-watcher site in your browser and copy the address from the address bar. The home page is fine; you don't need a port.
    2. 
        
  2. Open GrabMatlabLicense.ps1 in Notepad, find the LICENSE SERVER - fill these in block near the top, and paste the address between the quotes after $LicenseWebUrl =, for example [string]$LicenseWebUrl = 'http://licensewatcher/',.
    3. 
        
  3. Save it and restart the hunter. Within a minute the Server line shows MATLAB 8/10 in use, or a yellow error saying what went wrong.
    4. 
      

      
Way 2: lmutil (if the website ever goes away)

      
Put lmutil.exe (FlexLM's own tool, part of MathWorks' Network License Manager download) next to the script. The hunter finds the server by itself from MATLAB's license file. If it can't, the license-watcher home page lists the server as port@host (for example 27000@matlabls): put that in $LicenseServer.

TROUBLESHOOTING

  

    

      
SCRIPTS ARE BLOCKED

      
Your PC doesn't allow running .ps1 files. Use PasteIntoPowerShell.txt instead (see How to use). It runs the same hunter and makes a desktop shortcut that works on locked-down PCs.

    

    

      
WINDOW TOO NARROW

      
The dashboard needs a PowerShell window at least 118 columns wide. Maximize the window, or drag it wider, and it redraws on its own.

    

    

      
MATLAB NOT FOUND

      
The hunter looks for the newest MATLAB under C:\Program Files\MATLAB. If yours is somewhere else, start it with the path:

      
.\GrabMatlabLicense.ps1 -MatlabPath "D:\MATLAB\R2024b\bin\win64\MATLAB.exe"

    

    

      
THIS PAGE ISN'T UPDATING

      
This page reads LicenseData\dashboard-data.js, which is only rewritten while someone is running the hunter. If nobody is, the data goes stale. Click Load LicenseData folder at the top and pick the LicenseData folder to read the raw logs directly.

    

    

      
MATLAB CRASHES EVERY TIME IT STARTS

      
If MATLAB now shows the crash / "send feedback" screen every time it opens, its saved preferences have most likely been damaged (this can happen when MATLAB is closed hard while it is starting up). To fix it:

      
    
        
  1. Close MATLAB (and the hunter).
    2. 
        
  2. Press Win+R, type %APPDATA%\MathWorks\MATLAB and press Enter.
    3. 
        
  3. Rename the folder for your version, e.g. R2024b → R2024b_old.
    4. 
        
  4. Start MATLAB again. It rebuilds fresh preferences. (You can copy anything you miss, like favorites, back from the _old folder later.)
    5. 
      

      
The hunter kills a MATLAB that didn't get a seat (so retries are quick), and after every such kill it restores a snapshot of your preferences taken when the hunt started, so this should not keep happening.

      
Still crashing? It may be the graphics driver. Try starting MATLAB with -softwareopengl (add it to the end of the MATLAB shortcut's Target), or update your graphics driver.
